An example test course

Every test center has its own layout, but they are built from the same parts: crank, S-curve, hill start, railroad crossing, crosswalks, a 50 km/h straight and traffic lights. This example center has three courses and the examiner picks one on the day. Choose a course to see its route in the sheet's colors. Numbered pins mark the traps (purple 10 = lane change); tap any pin or list item for how to handle it. Press Play to watch the car drive it. Why the route is in three colors

The dotted red on Courses 1 and 3 is the test ride. It is not judged, so use it to get a feel for the car's brakes, accelerator and steering. Scoring starts where the line turns solid. Each color section also covers a block of tasks: red is the start and the crank, blue is the hill start and S-curve, green is the 50 km/h road, the traffic lights and the way back. Ask staff at your center how their course sheet is colored.

Traps and how to beat them

Numbered to match the pins on the map. Since October 2025, foreign-license conversion tests are scored like a brand-new license test, so every check has to be visible to the examiner.

Why good drivers fail: drive the test, not the road

A common story from experienced drivers who fail: the examiner says the driving was technically good, but too fast in the turns and too fast up and down the hill. The test doesn't measure how well you drive. It checks that you perform a set routine the examiner can see from the passenger seat. Good real-world habits like trail braking, a smooth line or keeping up with traffic don't earn points. If the examiner can't see the routine, the points are deducted.

  • Separate braking from steering. Do all your braking on the straight, with the wheels pointing ahead. By the time you start to turn the wheel, your foot should be off the brake and the car already slow. Trail braking (braking into the curve while steering) is good technique on the road and at the track. Here, the examiner sees it as arriving too fast and still correcting.
  • Under 20 km/h in every curve, including the big sweeping bends of the outer circuit, not only the 90° corners. Aim for 10–15 km/h (徐行, walking pace) at intersections, so even with a misjudgement you stay well under 20.
  • Steady through the curve. Keep one speed from turn-in to the exit, with no brake and no extra throttle. Add power only once the wheel is coming back straight.
  • Make it visible. Brake a little earlier and harder than feels necessary, so the slowdown is obvious. Look over your shoulder and at the mirrors with your head, not just your eyes.
  • Hill: climb slowly, stop next to the pole, restart without rolling back, and crawl down the other side (徐行: slow enough to stop at once) with your foot over the brake. The downhill is a curve-speed test too.
  • On the map: press Play and watch the km/h box. Red means braking with the wheels straight. Green means off the brake, under 20 km/h, steering. The car never brakes and steers at the same time.
  • Retake: book again with your test center. Many centers post the day's course in the morning and let you walk it at lunchtime while the course lights are off; ask at the counter. The knowledge test pass is valid for 6 months.

Instant fail (試験中止)

Any of these ends the test on the spot, whatever your score.

Not stopping at a stop sign / stop line (一時不停止) Entering on red or yellow (信号無視) Not stopping before a railroad crossing (踏切不停止) Driving on the right side (右側通行) Examiner touches brake or wheel (補助) Big wheel-off or hitting a pole (脱輪・接触 大) Rolling back a lot on the hill (逆行 大) A 4th reversal (切り返し) in one narrow section Score drops below 70

Routines to drill

Examiners in Japan mark the head movement, not the eyes. Turn your head so the examiner can see each check.

Before you drive

  1. Walk to the car from the rear. Glance under and around it, front and back.
  2. Before opening the door, look back for traffic (後方確認).
  3. Get in, close and lock the door. Seat, then mirrors, then seat belt.
  4. Foot on the brake, check the lever is in P, start the engine.

Pulling away (発進)

  1. Foot brake, select D.
  2. Right turn signal.
  3. Release the parking brake.
  4. Rear-view mirror, right mirror, then turn your head over the right shoulder (目視).
  5. About 3 seconds after signaling, move off smoothly.

Left turn (左折)

  1. About 30 m before: rear-view mirror, left mirror, left turn signal.
  2. Head check over the left shoulder for bikes (巻き込み確認).
  3. Move to within about 1 m of the left edge (左寄せ).
  4. Brake to walking pace (徐行, 10–15 km/h) before the corner, while still straight. Release the brake before you turn the wheel (no trail braking). Check left once more, then turn at one steady speed.
  5. Turn tight, staying close to the left curb.

Right turn (右折)

  1. About 30 m before: rear-view mirror, right mirror, right turn signal, right head check.
  2. Move over next to the center line.
  3. Brake to walking pace before the intersection, then release the brake before you steer. Stay under 20 km/h through the turn. Yield to oncoming traffic and to pedestrians on the far crosswalk.
  4. Turn just inside the center of the intersection. Do not cut the corner.
  5. If the new road has two lanes your way, finish the turn in the left lane (第一通行帯), not the right one. In the US you'd take the nearest lane; in Japan the law says keep to the leftmost lane, and the right lane is only for passing or a right turn coming up. The exception: if guide lines are painted through the intersection (for example two right-turn lanes side by side), follow your line into the lane it leads to. Otherwise take the right lane only if the examiner tells you to.

Lane change (進路変更)

  1. Rear-view mirror, then the door mirror on the side you're moving to.
  2. Turn signal on. Wait about 3 seconds before moving.
  3. Mirror again, then turn your head to check the blind spot (目視).
  4. Move over gradually, taking about 3 seconds. No sudden swerve.
  5. Turn signal off once you're settled.

Never change lanes inside an intersection, on or just before a crosswalk, or across a solid line. On this example course the only real lane change is before the right turn off the bottom road (trap 9), where the road has two lanes each way. Before the other right turns the road has one lane each way: just move next to the center line.

Stop sign (止まれ)

  1. Stop completely with the front bumper before the line. Count to 2.
  2. If you can't see, creep forward and stop again.
  3. Look right, left, right with big head turns, then go. On this example course: every time you cross the central 4-lane road away from the traffic lights.

Finishing at 発着点

  1. Mirror, left turn signal, left head check.
  2. Pull in parallel and close to the edge (about 30 cm), stop smoothly.
  3. Parking brake, P, turn signal off, engine off when asked.
  4. Before opening the door: mirror plus head check behind. Open it a little first.

What past test-takers report

From Japanese forums and guides. Experiences differ between test centers and examiners, so treat these as tips rather than rules.

Tips from test-takers

  • The course is usually chosen on the day from the posted courses. Learn all of them.
  • Where centers allow it, candidates walk the course at lunchtime. Posters say walking it beats studying the map.
  • One poster said the course feels narrower than a driving school's. Keep speed low in the crank and S-curve.
  • A wrong turn costs nothing by itself, but how you drive back onto the route is scored.
  • At green lights and priority roads, don't do extra intersection checks. One poster says too much head-turning there gets marked as not looking ahead (よそ見).
  • Drive with contrast (メリハリ): speed up on straights, slow clearly for curves and turns. Timid crawling everywhere also loses points.
  • Make checks exaggerated. One poster's word: "わざとらしく" (theatrically).
  • Examiners are police officers and differ in style. If you arrive early, watch how others drive.

About the national rules

  • Since October 2025 the foreign-license skill test follows the new-license scoring standard. The softer "warning only" category (事後注意) was removed.
  • Crosswalks, railroad crossing and hill start were added as set tasks. Course length is 1,200 m or more.
  • Reported pass rate for the skill test from Oct 2025 to Mar 2026 was about 13.7%, down from about 30% before. Most people fail the first time. Don't panic if you do.
  • Failure to check (安全不確認) costs 10 points each time. A badly executed turn costs 5.
  • Most common reasons for failing: late left-right checks, no blind-spot check on left turns, not moving over before turns, turn signals too early or too late.
  • Turn signal 30 m before a turn; 3 seconds before a lane change.
  • If you fail, you can retake the skill test without redoing the knowledge test, within 6 months of your knowledge-test date.
